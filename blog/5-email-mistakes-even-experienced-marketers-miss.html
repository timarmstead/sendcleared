5 Email Mistakes Even Experienced Marketers Miss (And How to Catch Them Before Your Client Does)

Nearly every marketer has sent an email they wish they could unsend — a broken merge tag sitting right in the subject line, a "Shop Now" button that goes nowhere, a footer address that violates CAN-SPAM without anyone noticing until a client asks about it.

These aren't rookie mistakes. They happen to experienced teams running polished campaigns, precisely because a human reviewer skims for tone and messaging — not for the specific, boring, easy-to-miss technical details that actually break an email.

The problem with "just proofreading it"

A quick read-through catches typos. It rarely catches a merge tag that renders correctly in one ESP's preview but breaks the moment it's actually sent, or a tracking pixel with no alt text sitting invisibly in the footer, or nine out of ten links missing UTM parameters because someone forgot to update the template.

"The issues that actually damage client trust are almost never about the writing. They're about the ten small technical checks nobody thinks to look for."

Five things worth checking on every single send

  • Unresolved merge tags. A stray %FIRSTNAME% or {{customer.name}} visible in the final email is one of the fastest ways to look careless — and it happens more than most agencies would like to admit.
  • Missing UTM parameters. Without them, there's no way to measure how a campaign actually performed. This is easy to miss since the links still work fine — they just can't be tracked.
  • Alt text on real content images. This matters for accessibility and for anyone with images blocked by default. It's worth noting that tracking pixels shouldn't be flagged here — an empty alt="" on a genuine content image is the real problem.
  • A missing physical address. Required under CAN-SPAM and GDPR alike, and one of the most common compliance gaps in an otherwise polished template.
  • A subject line that's actually an internal label. "Q3 Newsletter — DRAFT v2" or "Test Send — Ignore" occasionally slips through when a template gets duplicated and the subject line never gets updated.

Why this is hard to catch manually — and easy to automate

None of these five issues are hard to understand. What makes them slip through isn't complexity, it's that they require checking specific, unglamorous details on every single send, every single time, without ever getting complacent. That's exactly the kind of repetitive, detail-heavy work that's genuinely well-suited to automated checking — not replacing a human's judgment on tone and messaging, but catching the technical details a skim-read reliably misses.
